Splice body edits and read direct body items without the whole body

Paragraph.xml, insert_paragraph and remove_content cost a time in
proportion to the document body (#407), as did Run.xml, Table.xml,
Cell.xml, add_paragraph, insert_paragraph_before, remove_content_range,
insert_table and add_table. A read serialized and scanned the whole body
to find one item, after a linear search of the owner inventory and a
story enumeration. A body edit cloned the document, then rebuilt the
Python owner inventory from one body serialization, story scans and a
reparse of every paragraph.

A direct body paragraph, run, table or cell now serializes alone under
the main part root, found through the inventory index. Its XML is the
whole-source XML up to one visible byte change. The whole part declares
w14 on its root when any of its bytes use it, so a fragment used to carry
xmlns:w14 whenever any other body item did. A fragment, a cell included,
now declares w14 only when the part root does or the fragment uses it,
by name or in a markup compatibility list such as Requires="w14". Every
other declaration is unchanged.

insert_paragraph, Paragraph.insert_paragraph_before, add_paragraph,
insert_table and add_table without a style, and remove_content and
remove_content_range on plain direct paragraphs, now splice the owner
inventory in place, a comments part present or not. Kept owners move
their body routes and, after an inserted table, their cell story owners.
The inserted owners are born and the removed ones retire, with the
retirements, births and order the rebuild gives, so every handle keeps
its validity and its stale message. The publication is prepared before
the native edit, which then happens in place without a staged clone.
remove_content and remove_content_range remove a plain paragraph in
place in a document with comments too, since it holds no comment anchor.
A table inserted in a main part with a text box story keeps the rebuild,
since the inventory cannot place text box stories.

Documents that python-docx writes for review took none of these routes.
A paragraph inside a nested content control, such as a repeating section
item, has no story route, and the snippets python-docx appends declare w
again on a content control or a tracked change, which kept every local
edit on the staged path. The splice now counts facade paragraphs from
the body model and leaves an unrouted paragraph in place, and an
insertion before a slot that owns no item starts where the previous
slot's items end. A plain paragraph is a w:p owner and holds only w:r
owners, so removing or replacing one leaves the snapshots of every other
nested declaration owner intact: a local edit is now blocked only by a
shadowed serializer prefix or by a nested declaration on a w:p or a w:r.

Debug builds check every splice against the rebuild and every direct
read against the whole-source read, and count the rebuilds and
whole-source reads made outside those checks, which a test of the
binding uses to prove each call took its direct route.

A conservative publication still retires and rebirths the scopes of
every owner, so an edit stays linear in the owners, about 0.2 us each.
With body paragraphs of six runs, insert_paragraph takes 0.14, 1.28 and
5.51 ms at 100, 1,000 and 4,000 paragraphs, against 45, 471 and 2,297 ms
before, and a direct read takes about 0.1 ms at every size, against 13,
126 and 524 ms. On the rdocx-skills scaling documents of 25 and 100
paragraphs, with pictures, tables, comments, content controls and two
sections, the seven edits, add_heading and add_page_break take 0.06 to
0.43 ms and 0.11 to 0.57 ms, against 25 to 75 ms and 72 to 220 ms
before. Owner index maps hash with a word hasher, since their keys are
process-local integers.

RevisionCounter::prepare compared retired scopes pairwise, so retiring a
whole inventory was quadratic. It now keeps a set of their allocations.

Refs #407.
